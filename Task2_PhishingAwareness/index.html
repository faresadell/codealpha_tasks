What is phishing?

Phishing is a social engineering attack where someone impersonates a trusted source — a bank, a coworker, a delivery service — to trick you into handing over credentials, payment details, or access to a system. It rarely relies on technical hacking; it relies on you reacting before you think.

Most phishing falls into three shapes: mass phishing (generic, sent to thousands), spear phishing (personalized to you or your company), and smishing / vishing (the same trick over SMS or a phone call).

03

Social engineering tactics

Phishing works because it borrows tricks from everyday persuasion. Four show up again and again:

Urgency

Manufactured deadlines remove your time to think or verify.

"Your access expires in 1 hour — act now."

Authority

Impersonating a boss, IT department, or government agency to make refusal feel risky.

"This is IT — we need your password to complete the migration."

Fear

Threats of financial loss, legal trouble, or account suspension trigger a reflexive response.

"Unusual login detected. Confirm now or lose access."

Curiosity

A vague, intriguing message gets opened before it gets questioned.

"Someone shared a document with you."
04

Real-world examples

Phishing has cost real organizations real money — not because the technology was advanced, but because someone trusted a message they shouldn't have.

Business Email Compromise

Fake invoices, real wire transfers

Between 2013 and 2015, a scammer impersonated a hardware supplier and sent convincing fake invoices to two major tech companies, redirecting well over $100 million in payments to attacker-controlled accounts before the scheme was uncovered.

Credential Harvesting

The fake "shared document" link

A widely reported campaign sent messages that looked like a Google Docs sharing notification. Clicking the link led to a request for account permissions that, once granted, gave attackers access to the victim's contacts and inbox — which the malware then used to spread itself further.

Before you click, check three things

  • ✓ Sender: does the full email domain match the organization exactly — not just the display name?
  • ✓ Link: hover before you click. Does the real destination match what the text claims?
  • ✓ Pressure: is the message pushing you to act fast, skip a process, or keep it secret? That pressure is the tell.
